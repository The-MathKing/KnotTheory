# Episode 15 — What is proved, what is open, and how the numbers are trusted

Audience: has watched the series. This episode is the map of the whole project and its
standards of evidence.

## E15S01 — One grid, two directions

1. Here is the whole project in one picture. The n-th roots of unity, the grid that indexes the rotation symmetry. In Part One the matrix was fixed, its eigenvalues were two fixed curves sampled on that grid, and the question was whether the samples avoid a forbidden band: whether a fixed polynomial stays nonnegative. In Part Two the matrix ranged over everything with the pattern, and the question was how many roots of a polynomial you can choose to land on the same grid, and then, when the symmetry is broken, how to make the monodromy the identity for every n at once.

2. A fixed polynomial avoiding a band. A constructed polynomial hitting the grid. Same object, opposite direction. And the same exact-arithmetic machinery deciding both: reduce to cyclotomic points, certify signs and vanishing exactly, and never trust a floating-point number where a floating-point number cannot decide.

## E15S02 — Four standards of evidence

3. The paper ends with a status section that labels every result by the standard it meets, and the labels are different in kind. Standard one: proved, with a proof in the text that a reader can check line by line. Standard two: proved by exact certificate, meaning an explicit matrix whose nullity, or an explicit algebraic number whose sign, is computed in whole-number arithmetic that cannot round. Standard three: proved by exact certification, meaning a Krawczyk contraction test evaluated in exact fractions, which proves a true solution exists in a box without exhibiting it exactly. Standard four: proved finite, by exhaustive search over a finite set, with the search validated against independent methods.

4. And then two more labels: numerical only, for results from floating-point computations that were never made exact, stated as observations and never as theorems; and open, for things not known. The honesty of the project is in the labels, and in the fact that the labels moved when the evidence changed.

## E15S03 — The results, by standard

5. Proved in the text: the reduction to a recurrence and the ceiling theorem; the classification of rotation-invariant certificates by the symbol; the realisability criterion with its antipodal obstruction; the theorem that a fixed symbol covers only one divisibility class; the tiling theorem; the reductions for two-vertex bases; the K four refutation; and in Part One, the polynomial criterion, the corner form, the uniform bound, the Dirichlet bound, and the parity law.

6. Proved by exact certificate: k equals two for all n at least nine, except ten, by the rotation-invariant construction, and n equals ten by an integer matrix; k equals three on multiples of ten; k equals four on multiples of sixty, seventy, and ninety; k equals five on multiples of twenty-four; k equals seven on multiples of one hundred twenty; and the complete Ramanujan classification, four hundred sixty pairs.

7. Proved by exact certification: the all-large-n theorems. Z equals M equals eight for k equals three and n at least seventeen; ten for k equals four and n at least twenty-nine; twelve for k equals five and n at least one hundred sixty-two. Proved finite: every tabulated small value, including the complete rows for k equals two, three, four.

8. Numerical only, and said so: values at k equals six for seven values of n, and one at k equals eight, from Newton solves that were never certified. These appear as observations.

9. Open: whether identity tiles exist for k at least six, where parameter counting says length fifty-four and none have been found. Whether the maximum nullity of P of twenty-four, four is ten; this is the one value where the matrix statement is weaker than the forcing statement, since Z is known to be ten there but no matrix of nullity ten has been found, and a theorem proved late in the project shows no rotation-invariant matrix can reach it. The exact maximum nullity of the K four family, somewhere between n and n plus two. And the leading-coefficient conjecture.

10. Refuted, by the project itself: eight earlier claims, listed in the paper with dates, including the cubic gap conjecture and the regular-base ceiling; and one search withdrawn as unreliable, because it collapsed onto degenerate configurations and reported plateaus that were not there.

## E15S04 — Verification as method

11. A word on how the numbers are trusted, because for a project like this it is half the work. One script re-derives every computational claim from the certificates stored on disk: one hundred forty-one checks at the time of recording, taking about seven minutes. Every number in the paper, every threshold, every tile count, every census figure, is generated by that script and read into the document automatically, never typed by hand. The rule was adopted after the census drifted three times through copying errors.

12. Every search runs beside a control. The exact Ramanujan classifier ran beside a floating-point classifier, and the control found the k equals one bug. The nullity searches ran under the ceiling theorem, which caught two impossible answers. The exhaustive forcing solver was checked against brute force and against two independent formulations before being trusted on any new value.

13. And the log records wrong turns with dates. The invalid Taylor derivation of the uniform bound. A degree claimed as two k that was two k plus two. A misattributed citation. A separation guard that underflowed to zero and so could never fire. The pivoting that left twenty-eight equations unproved. The cubic gap conjecture. Each of these produced a sentence in the paper: a passing test is not a proof; a converged optimum is not a witness; a failed search measures effort, not impossibility. Each sentence was earned by a specific mistake.

## E15S05 — What the project found, in one breath

14. Exactly four hundred sixty generalized Petersen graphs satisfy the Riemann Hypothesis, none past k equals forty-five, by a criterion with no k in it and a bound of two hundred thirty on n. Z of P of n, k equals two k plus two for all n at least seventeen at k equals three and at least twenty-nine at k equals four, which with exhaustive search determines Z at every n for k equals two, three, and four, and proves Krishnan's Conjecture 5 with the best possible threshold, thirteen. A ceiling theorem saying the matrix method either solves the problem completely or cannot touch it. And a refuted conjecture, with the one-line reason why.

15. What is next is written down too. Close the one open value at k equals four. Find identity tiles at k equals six, or prove they do not exist below some length. Prove or refute the leading-coefficient criterion. Those are the open problems. This is where the project stands on the second of October, 2026.

## E15S06 — How to use this series

16. If you are the author, this series is a drill. Every worked example is meant to be redone by hand: the Petersen blocks, D two at n equals five, Q at n equals twenty-four, the kernel of the three-vertex path, the n equals twelve certificate, Krawczyk on root two, the rank of the K four matrix. If you can do each one on a whiteboard without notes, you can explain the project to anyone, including a judge who asks the one question you did not prepare for.

17. If you are not the author, this series is a map, and the paper is the territory. Every theorem named here is stated and proved there, with the status label attached. Where this series says a thing is true, the paper says how it is known. That difference is the point.
