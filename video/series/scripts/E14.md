# Episode 14 — Covers, the K4 family, and a refutation

Audience: knows P(n,k) and the ceiling idea. Voltage graphs, cyclic covers, and matrix
rank are built from scratch. This episode is about a mistake of the project's own.

## E14S01 — Building big graphs from small ones: voltage graphs

1. Here is a way to describe P of n, k with a picture that has only two vertices. Draw a vertex u and a vertex v. Draw a loop at u labelled one, a loop at v labelled k, and an edge from u to v labelled zero. The labels are called voltages, and the picture is called a voltage graph, or base graph.

2. The rule for unfolding it, for a chosen n: make n copies of each base vertex, u zero to u n minus one and v zero to v n minus one. For each base edge from x to y with voltage g, and each i, draw an edge from x i to y i plus g, with i plus g wrapped around modulo n. The loop at u with voltage one gives edges u i to u i plus one: the outer ring. The loop at v with voltage k gives v i to v i plus k: the inner pattern. The edge u to v with voltage zero gives u i to v i: the spokes. The unfolded graph is P of n, k.

3. The unfolded graph is called the cyclic cover of the base, or the n-fold cover, and it always has the rotation symmetry i to i plus one, by construction. The n copies of a base vertex are called its fibre. Any base graph, with any whole-number voltages on its edges, unfolds this way for every n. For example, a single vertex with one loop of voltage one unfolds to the n-cycle. Two vertices joined by three parallel edges with voltages zero, one, and two unfold to a bipartite cubic graph on two n vertices.

4. The entire machinery of this series, the two-by-two blocks, the monodromy, the ceiling, works for any base graph. The blocks become b by b where b is the number of base vertices, built from the base's adjacency with each edge of voltage g contributing zeta to the g at the appropriate position. The project calls this matrix the symbol of the cover, written M of zeta.

## E14S02 — The general ceiling, and the regular-base claim

5. For P of n, k the ceiling on nullity was two k plus two. For a general base, the ceiling the project proved for rotation-invariant matrices is read off the determinant of M of zeta: it is a polynomial in zeta, and the bound is its degree span, the difference between the highest and lowest power of zeta appearing. For P of n, k that span is two k plus two. It depends on the base and voltages, not on n.

6. Now the story. The project went looking for a base graph where this ceiling is small but the zero forcing number grows with n. That would be a family of graphs with Z much larger than M, which would answer a question from a well-known survey about how far apart Z and M can be on cubic graphs. And it found a candidate.

7. The base is K four, the complete graph on four vertices, numbered zero to three, every pair joined. Voltages: zero on the edges zero-one, zero-three, and one-three; one on zero-two and two-three; two on one-two. The cover is cubic, connected, on four n vertices. The degree span of the symbol is six for every n. An explicit integer matrix attains nullity six. Meanwhile an exact search found Z equals n plus two for n from four to eight. Z grows with n; the rotation-invariant ceiling sits at six.

8. The project concluded that the maximum nullity of this family is six, that the gap Z minus M grows without bound, conjectured that this holds for cubic graphs in general, and put it on the poster and in the interview script as the strongest result in the project. It was called the cubic gap conjecture.

## E14S03 — Rank, in one example

9. To see what went wrong we need the notion of rank. Take a vector u equals one, two, three, and form the three-by-three matrix u u transpose, whose entry in row i and column j is u i times u j. Rows: one, two, three; two, four, six; three, six, nine. Every row is a multiple of the first row. Every column is a multiple of the first column. The matrix has only one independent row. Its rank is one.

10. The rank of a matrix is the number of independent rows, equivalently the number of independent columns. For an N by N matrix, rank plus nullity equals N: the directions the matrix does not kill, plus the directions it kills, make up everything. So a three-by-three matrix of rank one has nullity two. Check: u u transpose times the vector two, minus one, zero gives row one dotted: two minus two is zero; row two: four minus four, zero; row three: six minus six, zero. And three, zero, minus one works too. Two independent kernel vectors.

11. The important thing: u u transpose has every off-diagonal entry nonzero when every u i is nonzero. So on a triangle, three vertices all joined, u u transpose is a legal matrix with the triangle's pattern, since the diagonal is free. And it has rank one, the smallest possible for a nonzero matrix. A triangle can carry a rank-one block.

## E14S04 — The refutation

12. Now read the voltages of the K four base again. The three edges with voltage zero are zero-one, zero-three, and one-three. They form a triangle on the base vertices zero, one, three. In the cover, a voltage-zero edge joins x i to y i, same index. So for each i, the three vertices zero i, one i, and three i form a triangle in the cover. n triangles, one per fibre index. And the only edges leaving a triangle go to the fibre over vertex two, because every other base edge has one end at vertex two.

13. Build a matrix. On each of the n triangles, put a rank-one block u u transpose with u equals one, two, three. On the edges to the fibre over vertex two, put ones. On the diagonal at vertex two's fibre, put ones. This is a legal matrix for the cover: every edge gets a nonzero entry, the diagonal is free.

14. Now count the rank. The three n rows belonging to triangle vertices split into two parts: a part on the triangle columns, which is block diagonal with n rank-one blocks, so rank at most n; and a part on the n columns of vertex two's fibre, rank at most n. So those three n rows have rank at most two n. The remaining n rows, at vertex two's fibre, have rank at most n. Total rank at most three n. The matrix is four n by four n. So the nullity is at least four n minus three n, which is n.

15. Exact computation over the rationals confirms it: for n from four to ten, the nullity is exactly n. The maximum nullity of the K four family is at least n, not six. The gap Z minus M is at most two, not growing. The cubic gap conjecture is false, and K four is cubic, so the stronger claim that any regular base has a bounded ceiling is false too.

## E14S05 — What was actually wrong

16. Where was the error? The ceiling theorem for general covers bounds the nullity of rotation-invariant matrices by the degree span of the determinant of M of zeta. The rank-one matrix is rotation-invariant, so the theorem should apply. But look at its symbol: because of the rank-one blocks, the determinant of M of zeta is identically zero, the zero polynomial. The degree span of the zero polynomial is undefined, and the theorem's proof silently assumed the determinant was not identically zero. That hypothesis was true for P of n, k and for every base the project had tested, and it was never written down. The theorem was true with the hypothesis and wrong without it.

17. The second lesson is about search. The project had run two adversarial searches for high-nullity matrices on this family, and both missed this matrix, because it lives where three of the four fibre blocks are singular, and neither search goes near such degenerate points. The counterexample was found by reading the voltages and noticing the triangle, not by any search. A failed search measures the effort spent, not the impossibility of what it failed to find.

18. What replaces the regular-base claim is a conjecture about the leading coefficient of the symbol: on every base where the ceiling holds, the top coefficient of the determinant of M of zeta is a product of edge weights only; on every base where it fails, that coefficient involves a diagonal entry. That pattern is checked on all ten bases in the paper and proved on three families, and it is stated as a conjecture, not a theorem. It is the one thing in the project that an expert might call interesting, and it is honestly labelled open.

## E14S06 — Why this episode exists

19. This is the episode about the project's own mistake, and it is here on purpose. The clearest thing a research record can show is a false claim caught and corrected by its author, in writing, with the reason. The date is the first of October, 2026. The poster, the abstract, and the paper were all corrected the same day, and the log records what the claim was, who found the problem, and why the proof had a hole. If you only remember one thing from Part Two, remember that the headline claim was wrong, that the reason was a triangle hiding in three zeros, and that the fix was a hypothesis the theorem had needed all along.

## E14S07 — Recap

20. What you should now be able to do. Define a voltage graph and unfold it for a given n. Show that the two-vertex base with voltages one, k, zero unfolds to P of n, k. Define rank, compute the rank of u u transpose, and state rank plus nullity equals N. Read the K four voltages and find the triangle. Build the rank-one matrix and count its rank to get nullity at least n. State what hypothesis the cover theorem was missing. And explain why two searches missed the counterexample.
