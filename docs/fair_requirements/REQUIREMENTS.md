# Fair requirements — TXSEF and ISEF

Pulled 2026-09-27 from official sources. Raw extracts alongside:
`isef2026_display_guidelines.txt`, `txsef2024_finalist_guide.txt`.

**Caveat on currency.** The ISEF text is the 2026 guidelines; the TXSEF text is
the 2024 finalist guide, the most recent published. TXSEF states the 2027 rules
are not yet out and to use 2026 in the interim. Re-pull both before relying on
any deadline or fee. DRSEF (the regional, Feb 2027) has not been pulled and its
rules govern first.

---

## The single most important number

Both fairs use the same 100-point rubric. Its weights:

| Criterion | Points |
|---|---|
| Research Question | 10 |
| Design and Methodology | 15 |
| Execution: data collection, analysis, interpretation | 20 |
| Creativity | 20 |
| **Presentation — poster** | **10** |
| **Presentation — interview** | **25** |

**The poster is worth 10 points. The interview is worth 25.** Poster criteria are
only: *logical organization of material, clarity of graphics and legends,
supporting documentation displayed.* Nothing about density, elegance or
typography. Effort spent past "organized and legible" has a low ceiling.

Interview criteria, by contrast, include *understanding interpretation and
limitations of results*, *degree of independence*, and *quality of ideas for
further research* — all of which this project is unusually well placed on,
because the log documents exactly that.

## ISEF — three separate artifacts

Judges primarily read the **virtual Project Presentation**, not the board:
*"the virtual project presentation is the primary way to present the content of
your project."*

### 1. Project Presentation (the judged artifact)
- Up to **12 landscape pages**, each uploaded as a **separate image** (JPG/PNG).
- Body text **minimum 14 pt, 18 pt recommended**; captions may go to 12 pt.
- No animation, no active hyperlinks.
- Must follow the **Mathematics/Computer Science template** and *"do not include
  information not specified in this template."* Each section starts on its own
  page, in this order:
  1. Project ID and Title
  2. **INTRODUCTION** — research question; what is known; literature review
  3. **FRAMEWORK** — notation and definitions needed to state the question precisely
  4. **FINDINGS** — *"rigorous proofs of the theory results — or, if the arguments
     are long, give sketches of the proofs that explain the main ideas"*
  5. **CONCLUSIONS** — how results address the question; limitations; unexpected
     problems; applications if any
  6. **REFERENCES / ACKNOWLEDGEMENTS** — max one page, IEEE/APA/etc, DOIs

  This is why the 2026 first-place math board reads like a typeset paper with a
  visible "Proof Sketch": the template asks for proofs.

### 2. Quad Chart / Visual Summary (public-facing)
- **One landscape page**, four equal quadrants with a single border line each
  (or up to 5 clearly labelled areas).
- **Minimum 18 pt**, light background, dark text, bulleted and brief.
- Quadrants: Research Question / Methodology / Data Analysis and Results / (4th).
- No bibliography, no references, no acknowledgements, may not use the word
  "abstract".

### 3. Physical display board
- Max **48 in wide × 94.5 in tall × 29.9 in deep** (122 × 240 × 76 cm), including
  table and any title board.
- Suggested **total word count 300–800 words** — a guideline ISEF imports from
  NYU, not a hard rule, and visibly not what top math boards do.
- Title short and interest-drawing; section headers, bullets, colour, graphics.

### Prohibited on any ISEF display
- Contact information of any kind: email, phone, **social media, QR codes**
- **Commercial logos, institutional crests, brand references**, self-promotion
  or external endorsement
- Published research papers (except inside a closed lab notebook)
- Handouts: brochures, business cards, drives
- Active internet connections without prior approval
- Graphics need citations: own work needs software + year; others' need full APA
  with URL; AI-generated needs tool and date

### Also required
- Official Abstract, **250 words**, SRC-approved: research question, procedures,
  data, interpretation, conclusions.
- Project video, **2 minutes maximum**, finalist prominently on screen, public.

## TXSEF

- Physical board max **48 in wide × 30 in deep × 108 in tall** (taller and deeper
  than ISEF; the 48 in width is the same).
- **Virtual display board** (PDF) — *"represents the project poster used during
  the in-person fair"* — plus the SRC-approved abstract from STEM Wizard.
- **Project overview video, 2 minutes maximum.** Judges have access but
  *"it will not be the focus of their project review."*
- Virtual boards must obey the same Display & Safety rules as physical ones.
- In-person category judging at Texas A&M, interviewed in person.
- Two platforms: **STEM Wizard** for SRC approval, then **OpenWater** for TXSEF
  registration. SRC approval must complete *before* registering.
- Fee **$50 per student**, no onsite payment, no personal checks.

## What this implies for this project

1. **The board is not where the marginal point is.** 10 points, and its stated
   criteria are organization and legibility. The current board already clears
   that bar.
2. **Build the 12-page Presentation to the math template.** It is the primary
   judged artifact at ISEF, its six sections map directly onto the paper, and
   it explicitly wants proofs — which is the project's strength. It is also
   reusable as the TXSEF virtual board.
3. **Check the board against the prohibitions.** The current header names the
   sponsoring regional fair, which is plausibly a "brand reference"; worth
   removing or confirming.
4. **The 250-word abstract and the 2-minute video are both required and neither
   exists yet.**
5. **Pull DRSEF's rules** — that fair is first and its rules, not these, govern
   in February.
